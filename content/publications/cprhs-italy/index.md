abstract: |
  We provide the first application of a Quantitative Spatial Model to the Italian economy. We extend Caliendo, Parro, Rossi-Hansberg, and Sarte's (2018) framework — a multi-region multi-sector model with input-output linkages — by allowing for international trade, and estimate it for Italy. To showcase the model's potential, we use it to study three scenarios: (i) the 2016 earthquake in central Italy; (ii) a reduction in trade costs, consistent with the EU Transport White Paper; and (iii) a shutdown of trade between China and Western countries. The first experiment uncovers large GDP losses in affected regions and a significant expansion of economic activity in the nearby regions stemming from labor reallocation. Second, we find that lower shipping costs improve firms' input selection, thus increasing aggregate GDP. Third, the shutdown of trade between China and Western countries triggers the substitution of foreign inputs with domestic inputs of lower quality, thereby depressing aggregate GDP, especially in Northern regions, which are more exposed to international trade. Our results highlight the importance of considering general equilibrium effects related to changes in trade linkages and labor reallocation across regions.

summary: A multi-region, multi-sector quantitative spatial model of the Italian economy with international trade, applied to three policy experiments.
