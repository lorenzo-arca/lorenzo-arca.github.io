abstract: |
  This paper develops a single-sector open-economy model in which the only source of firm-level market power is on the input side. Firms produce a homogeneous good sold in a perfectly competitive product market, hire workers in oligopsonistic local labor markets with nested-CES labor supply, and combine labor with foreign intermediate inputs in a CES bundle inside a decreasing-returns technology. The single firm-level trade margin is the decision to pay a fixed cost and source inputs from abroad; foreign demand for the domestic good enters only in the aggregate, through an Armington-style export demand curve, so there is no firm-level export decision by construction. The structure delivers three sharp objects. First, a transparent rent accounting: variable profits decompose exactly into decreasing-returns quasi-rents, monopsony rents on labor, and buyer-power rents on foreign inputs, with no markup component, and, for non-sourcing firms, the labor share of revenue equals the markdown times a technology constant. Second, the offshoring–monopsony link in its purest form: the markdown wedge raises the imported-input intensity of sourcing firms with elasticity ξ and lowers the productivity cutoff for paying the sourcing fixed cost. Third, a two-equation general equilibrium in the aggregate wage index and the domestic price, anchored by the foreign block. Calibrated to the universe of French manufacturing in 2003 — matching the observed local-labor-market structure establishment by establishment — the model implies substantial monopsony power, and the counterfactuals quantify the paper's central tension: access to foreign inputs is worth about 6 percent of real consumption to this economy, and the same margin depresses the aggregate labor share by 3.7 percentage points.
